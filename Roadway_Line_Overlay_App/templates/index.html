Roadway Overlay Matching Parameters & Geometric Logic Guide

Parameter Breakdown & Engineering Rationale

1. Buffer Distance (ft)
What it is: The lateral search corridor (width ±Buffer) generated around the target centerline.
Why it is needed: Roadways digitized across different GIS layers (e.g. FDOT Work Program vs. RCI roadway base maps) rarely share identical coordinates. Divided freeways have physical medians (50–300 ft apart), and GPS centerlines have digitizing jitter. The buffer ensures the reference line falls within the analytical search window.
2. Min Overlap Length (ft)
What it is: The minimum shared longitudinal length that the reference line must run along the target corridor.
Why it is needed: Perpendicular cross-streets, overpasses, and turning ramps will briefly intersect the buffer corridor for 50–100 ft. Requiring a substantial minimum overlap (e.g. 300 ft) discards accidental intersection touches.
3. Min Target Ratio (%)
What it is: The minimum proportion of the target feature's total length that must be covered (L_overlap / L_target × 100).
Why it is needed: Ensures proportional match significance. A 300 ft overlap on a 5-mile (26,400 ft) segment is only 1.1% (a minor clip), whereas 300 ft on a 400 ft bottleneck is 75% (a major corridor match).
4. Max Angle Delta (°)
What it is: The maximum angular difference between the directional bearing vector of the target line and the reference line (Δθ = |θ_target - θ_ref| mod 180°).
Why it is needed: Even if a cross-street passes through the buffer, its bearing is perpendicular (Δθ ≈ 90°). The bearing filter eliminates cross-streets while accommodating gentle highway curves and merges (≤ 30°).
5. Parallel Fallback (≥75% Overlap & ≤30ft Distance)
What it is: A dedicated secondary heuristic for divided highway centerlines and inverted digitizing directions.
Why it is needed: In many state DOT datasets, Southbound lanes may have been digitized pointing Northward, or tight parallel carriageways may exhibit micro-angle discrepancies on curves. When lines share ≥75% overlap and stay within 30 ft, they are guaranteed to represent the same corridor.

Recommended Settings Reference Table

Parameter Default Recommended Range When to Adjust
Buffer (ft) 300 ft 100 – 500 ft Increase for wide Interstate medians (e.g. 500 ft) or decrease for dense downtown grids (e.g. 100 ft).
Min Overlap (ft) 300 ft 100 – 1,000 ft Increase to ignore short turn-lane connectors or ramps; decrease for short intersection bottlenecks.
Min Target Ratio (%) 30% 20% – 60% Increase to require substantial corridor coverage; decrease if target segments are long multi-mile arcs.
Max Angle Delta (°) 30° 15° – 45° Keep at 30° for general highways. Increase to 45° for curved cloverleaf interchange ramps.
CRS (Projection) EPSG:26917 UTM 17N / 16N / State Plane Use EPSG:26917 for Central/South FL, EPSG:26916 for FL Panhandle, or State Plane (ft).
